Physics & data simulation

Observational context

LISA observes the milliHertz band (approximately $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-1}$ Hz). The pipeline operates at the cadence of the LISA Data Challenge level-1 products, 0.2 Hz ($\Delta t = 5$ s), on signals lasting hours to weeks. Telemetry arrives as time-delay interferometry (TDI) channels $X, Y, Z$; the pre-processor forms the orthogonal channel $A = (Z - X)/\sqrt{2}$ and operates on it exclusively.

Noise model (src/simulation.jl)

The strain-noise sensitivity is the sky- and polarisation-averaged model of Robson, Cornish & Liu, Class. Quantum Grav. 36 105011 (2019) [2]:

\[S_n(f) = \frac{10}{3 L^2} \left[ P_\mathrm{OMS}(f) + 2 \left(1 + \cos^2 \frac{f}{f_*}\right) \frac{P_\mathrm{acc}(f)}{(2\pi f)^4} \right] \left[ 1 + \frac{6}{10} \left(\frac{f}{f_*}\right)^2 \right] + S_c(f),\]

with $L = 2.5 \times 10^9$ m, $f_* = c/(2\pi L)$, the optical-metrology term $P_\mathrm{OMS} = (1.5 \times 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m})^2 [1 + (2\,\mathrm{mHz}/f)^4]$ Hz⁻¹, the acceleration term $P_\mathrm{acc} = (3 \times 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}})^2 [1 + (0.4\,\mathrm{mHz}/f)^2][1 + (f/8\,\mathrm{mHz})^4]$ Hz⁻¹, and the galactic confusion foreground

\[S_c(f) = A f^{-7/3} e^{-f^\alpha + \beta f \sin(\kappa f)} \left[ 1 + \tanh\left(\gamma (f_k - f)\right) \right], \qquad A = 9 \times 10^{-45}\,\mathrm{Hz^{-1}},\]

whose parameters depend on the observation time over which resolvable binaries are assumed subtracted (Table 1 of the reference; 0.5, 1, 2, or 4 years, selected by observation_years). lisa_noise_psd returns $S_n(f)$ in Hz⁻¹; instrument_psd and confusion_psd expose the two parts. The same function serves the simulator, the matched filter, and the whitening of features, so the three are consistent by construction.

Simulated telemetry (scripts/generate_data.jl)

The simulator produces a continuous strain record at physical amplitude:

  • Gaussian noise synthesised in the frequency domain against $S_n(f)$ (synthesize_noise): the spectral coefficients are drawn as complex normals scaled to $E|X_k|^2 = S_n(f_k) f_s n / 2$ in the unnormalised FFT convention, so the inverse transform has the correct one-sided PSD and absolute amplitude. The confusion foreground is therefore part of the Gaussian noise. Bins below noise_f_min_hz (default $10^{-5}$ Hz, the low end of the band over which the sensitivity model is defined) are not synthesised: the model's extrapolation towards zero frequency would introduce into the record a drift many orders of magnitude above the in-band level.
  • Resolvable galactic binaries: monochromatic sinusoids with random frequencies in 0.1–10 mHz; EMRIs: three-harmonic chirps with a constant frequency drift. Each is scaled to a matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio drawn from the configured range (gb_snr_min/max, emri_snr_min/max), evaluated over the simulated record.
  • MBHB injections: the non-spinning inspiral–merger–ringdown model IMRPhenomA of Ajith et al., Phys. Rev. D 77 104017 (2008) [3], with the coefficients of its Table I (src/waveforms.jl). The frequency-domain amplitude is $(f/f_\mathrm{merg})^{-7/6}$ in the inspiral, $(f/f_\mathrm{merg})^{-2/3}$ up to $f_\mathrm{ring}$, and a Lorentzian of width $\sigma$ centred on $f_\mathrm{ring}$ up to $f_\mathrm{cut}$, with the transition frequencies $(a\eta^2 + b\eta + c)/(\pi M)$ set by the detector-frame total mass $M$ (drawn log-uniformly in [mbhb_total_mass_min, mbhb_total_mass_max]; the source-frame mass of [4], §2.3, is $M/(1+z)$) and the symmetric mass ratio $\eta$ (mass ratio uniform in [1, mbhb_mass_ratio_max]); the phase is the polynomial $\sum_k \psi_k (\pi M f)^{(k-5)/3}$. Each waveform is generated on the sampling grid of its injection segment: the ringdown frequency is placed at the target merger time through the phase's group delay, inspiral content that would arrive before the segment start is rolled off, and the spectrum is tapered to zero at nyquist_taper times the Nyquist frequency, so the sampled waveform cannot alias, irrespective of the mass. The waveform is then aligned so that its amplitude peak falls on the drawn merger sample $k_c$ (place_signal!), truncated to the record, and scaled to a matched-filter SNR $\rho$ drawn from [snr_min, snr_max] (default $[8, 50]$):

\[\rho^2 = 4 \int_0^\infty \frac{|\tilde h(f)|^2}{S_n(f)} \, \mathrm{d}f \approx 4 \Delta f \sum_{k \ge 1} \frac{|\tilde h(f_k)|^2}{S_n(f_k)}.\]

Point-wise labels mark, by default, the detectable span of each injection (label_span = "detectable"): the union of the windows of label_window_size samples whose matched-filter SNR against $S_n$ reaches label_snr_threshold (detectable_span), i.e. the samples over which an optimal filter on single windows detects the signal. Early-inspiral samples whose window SNR is below the threshold are indistinguishable from noise and are labelled as such. The alternatives are every injected sample ("injection") and the fixed span $[t_c - \texttt{label\_before\_sec},\, t_c + \texttt{label\_after\_sec}]$ ("fixed"). The per-sample SNR column carries the event's total $\rho$. Output is an HDF5 file (obs/tdi/{t, X, Z}, with $X \equiv 0$ and $Z = \sqrt{2}\,A$ so the pre-processor round-trips; the two-channel layout of the constellation response is given below), a point-wise label CSV, an event catalogue CSV (merger sample and time, $\rho$, waveform parameters, covered and labelled sample ranges, and the signal onset signal_start_index, the first window before the coalescence whose SNR reaches label_snr_threshold, from which a streamed replay credits alerts), and a generation snapshot with the seed and the hardware fingerprint.

Constellation response (ext/MilliHertzQMLCurvatureDistinguishabilityExt.jl)

The record above refers one strain to the sky-averaged sensitivity and places every source at a matched-filter SNR. With [generation] response = "lisa" the simulator records instead the two noise-orthogonal channels $A$ and $E$ of the constellation, through the detector response of CurvatureDistinguishability.jl [5], loaded as a package extension (using CurvatureDistinguishability; the interface is declared in src/response.jl). The extension supplies only the response: the antenna patterns of the three Michelson interferometers on the analytic equal-arm orbits of Rubbo, Cornish & Poujade [6], combined into $A = (Z - X)/\sqrt{2}$ and $E = (X - 2Y + Z)/\sqrt{6}$ and rescaled so that each channel's $F_+^2 + F_\times^2$ averages to $3/10$ over sky and polarisation, the orbital Doppler phase $\Delta_D = 2\pi f\, \hat n \cdot \vec R_c(t)/c$ of the constellation centre, and the long-wavelength transfer roll-off $\mathcal{T}(f) = [1 + 0.6 (f/f_*)^2]^{-1/2}$ of Robson et al. [2]. The waveform remains IMRPhenomA, here at physical amplitude: the unit spectrum of phenoma_spectrum is scaled by $\sqrt{5/24}\, \pi^{-2/3}\, \mathcal{M}^{5/6} f_\mathrm{merg}^{-7/6} / d_L$ (phenoma_physical_amplitude; Ajith et al. [3], Eq. 4.17), the plus-polarisation amplitude of a face-on source, for a luminosity distance drawn log-uniformly in [mbhb_distance_min_gpc, mbhb_distance_max_gpc], with the sky position, inclination and polarisation isotropic (draw_extrinsic). Each frequency of the spectrum is emitted $g(f) - g(f_\mathrm{ring})$ seconds before the coalescence (phenoma_arrival_delay, from the group delay of the fitted phase) and is projected with the antenna patterns and the Doppler phase of that instant:

\[\tilde h_C(f) = \mathcal{T}(f)\, e^{i\Delta_D(f, t_f)} \left[ \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \cos^2\iota)\, F^+_C(t_f) - i \cos\iota\, F^\times_C(t_f) \right] H(f), \qquad t_f = t_c - \max\{g(f) - g(f_\mathrm{ring}),\, 0\},\]

the leading order of the Fourier-domain response of Marsat & Baker [7], which is the response used for LISA massive-binary inference in Marsat, Baker & Dal Canton [8]. Through the merger–ringdown, where the derivative of the fitted phase is no longer an arrival time, the patterns are held at the coalescence; the yearly orbit does not resolve the difference. The cross polarisation lags the plus polarisation by a quarter cycle, hence the $-i$ in the $e^{-2\pi i f t}$ convention of the transform. Galactic binaries and EMRIs are projected in the time domain, $h_+ = a\, \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + \cos^2\iota) \cos(\Phi + \Delta_D)$ and $h_\times = a \cos\iota \sin(\Phi + \Delta_D)$ with the roll-off at the instantaneous frequency, each EMRI harmonic at its own frequency, and scaled to a network SNR, the quadrature sum over $A$ and $E$. The sky position $(\lambda, \beta)$ and the polarisation angle $\psi$ follow the ecliptic-frame conventions of [4] as CurvatureDistinguishability constructs them: the reference vectors $u = -e_\lambda$, $v = e_\beta$ of its Eq. (43), the wave-frame vectors $p = u \cos\psi + v \sin\psi$, $q = -u \sin\psi + v \cos\psi$ of Eq. (48), and the polarisation tensors $e^+ = p \otimes p - q \otimes q$, $e^\times = p \otimes q + q \otimes p$ of Eq. (62).

Each channel receives independent Gaussian noise of one-sided PSD $R(f)\, S_n(f)$, with $R(f) = \tfrac{3}{10}[1 + 0.6 (f/f_*)^2]^{-1}$ the sky- and polarisation-averaged response of Robson et al. (sky_averaged_response, channel_noise_psd): the Michelson-channel level at which the antenna pattern is applied explicitly. A face-on source at a sky position of average pattern therefore has the sensitivity-curve SNR in one channel, and the average over sky and polarisation of the channel SNR² equals the sensitivity SNR², which the test suite checks by Monte Carlo. The confusion foreground enters through $S_n$ and is scaled with the instrument noise; the upstream noise model is not used, because it adds the sensitivity-level confusion fit of Robson et al. (Eq. 14) to the channel-level instrument noise without the factor $R(f)$. The pre-processor whitens such records with psd = "channel" (that PSD) or "welch".

The detectable-span labels and the catalogue snr follow label_channel: the $A$ channel the pipeline analyses (the default), $E$, or the network; the catalogue also records the distance, sky position, inclination, polarisation and both channel SNRs. The HDF5 record holds $X = -A/\sqrt{2} + E/\sqrt{6}$, $Y = -2E/\sqrt{6}$ and $Z = A/\sqrt{2} + E/\sqrt{6}$, so that tdi_to_aet returns $A$, $E$ and a null channel $T$ that vanishes identically: $T$ carries neither signal nor, in this model, noise. The limits of the model are those of its ingredients: the antenna patterns are the long-wavelength ones, valid for $f \ll f_* \approx 19$ mHz while merger frequencies reach ≈ 40 mHz at $10^5\,M_\odot$, where the roll-off approximates the full transfer function; the noise is the Michelson-channel model, not a TDI PSD; the arm length is the 2.5 Gm of the noise model; and the waveform remains non-spinning and dominant-mode.

Feature extraction (src/data.jl)

The record is first high-passed in the frequency domain with a zero-phase Butterworth magnitude response (highpass_record; default cutoff 0.5 mHz, order 8), because the acceleration noise rises as $f^{-6}$ below 0.4 mHz and the resulting sub-window drift would leak into every analysis band. The record is then whitened in the frequency domain by the model PSD (whiten_record): $X_k \to X_k \sqrt{2 / (f_s S_n(f_k))}$, so that noise following $S_n$ becomes white with unit variance. A sliding window (default 1000 samples, step 100) is advanced over the whitened $A$ channel; each window is Hann-tapered and its periodogram normalised by the taper's mean square (tapered_periodogram), $P_k = |\mathrm{FFT}(x v)_k|^2 / (n \overline{v^2})$, which has unit mean for noise. Whitening the record rather than each window keeps the tapered periodogram unbiased irrespective of the PSD slope: the periodogram of a tapered window is the PSD smoothed by the taper's main lobe, which is exact only on a flat spectrum. Single-window whitening remains possible for streaming use, where no record context exists. Both filters are circular: the record's ends are discontinuous when wrapped, and the whitening filter's impulse response is long when the PSD carries sharp features (the TDI null, the anti-aliasing roll-off), so the whitened power rings over a stretch at each end of the record — about 12 hours on the Sangria records, where the high-band power of the first and last ≈ 90 windows exceeds the noise range. Those windows are not validly conditioned; edge_margin (in window lengths, [preprocessing]) drops them from the product at each end, the sidecar records first_window so that mission times stay correct, and the metrics are computed on the interior only. Four features follow from $P_k$:

  1. mean whitened power in the low band (default 1–5 mHz);
  2. mean whitened power in the high band (default 5–100 mHz);
  3. spectral entropy of the normalised whitened power, divided by $\ln N_\mathrm{bins}$ (in $[0, 1]$);
  4. $\log_{10}$ of the standard deviation of the whitened power.

The band edges and the whitening PSD are configuration keys of [preprocessing]: psd = "model" is $S_n$ above (simulator products), "ldc" the analytic TDI PSD of the next section (LDC products), "welch" a median-averaged Welch estimate of the record itself (welch_psd; robust to the transient signals it contains and to the galactic foreground, which it absorbs into the whitening), and "none" no whitening. Because the features are PSD-normalised, they are independent of the window length and of the absolute amplitude of the channel.

A Welch estimate carries the scatter of neighbouring frequency bins and resolves sharp spectral features, the TDI transfer notch among them, and the inverse square root of such a spectrum has a long, ringing impulse response: whitening by it spreads every sample over many window lengths of the record. psd_smoothing_dex (default 0, off) smooths the estimate in log-frequency before whitening (smooth_psd): $\log_{10} S$ is averaged with Gaussian weights of that standard deviation in $\log_{10} f$, on a logarithmic grid, and read back at the frequencies of the estimate. At 0.01 dex, narrower than any analysis band, the envelope of the conditioning kernel at one window length falls from 21 % to 0.1 % of its peak, so that the features of a window depend on a short stretch of the record around it; the streamed replay needs correspondingly less context, and loses correspondingly less of the record to a delivery hole. The smoothing under-whitens the band around the notch near 60 mHz, and the features it produces differ from the unsmoothed ones, so a model must be trained on the whitening it is applied with (see the benchmark page).

The alternative feature set of Isfan et al. (2025) [1], feature_set = "paper", takes the normalised spectral entropy and the logarithm of the mean, standard deviation, and maximum of the periodogram of the raw window (the paper applies min–max scaling to the raw moments; the logarithm keeps the scaling well conditioned over the decades a TDI spectrum spans). It is meant for parity runs with psd = "none".

feature_set = "bands" generalises the whitened set to an arbitrary partition of the spectrum: band_edges_hz lists ascending edges, and the features are the mean whitened power of every band between consecutive edges (the first band closed on both sides, the others open at their lower edge), followed by the entropy and the log power spread — one more feature than there are edges, so n_qubits under [model] must equal that count. With the edges [1, 5, 100] mHz the set reproduces the whitened one exactly. Finer bands have a physical motivation: four days before merger the (2,2) frequency of a $10^6\,M_\odot$ binary is $\approx 0.4$ mHz and enters the 1–5 mHz band only in the last day, whereas a $10^5\,M_\odot$ binary is already at $\approx 1.6$ mHz, so a partition reaching below 1 mHz gives the classifier the early inspiral of the heavy systems, which the two-band set does not capture.

Features are mapped onto the phase-encoding interval $[0, \phi]$ — $\phi$ = [training] phase_span $\times \pi$, by default $\pi$, since the $R_z$ gate is $2\pi$-periodic and the full period would encode a saturated feature as one at the floor — by a FeatureScaler whose bounds are per-feature quantiles of the training partition (fit_scaler, [training] scaler_quantiles). The scaler is persisted inside the model artifact and applied unchanged at inference (encode_features), so no statistic of the evaluated data enters the encoding.

LISA Data Challenge products (src/ldc.jl)

LDC time series are TDI Michelson variables $X, Y, Z$ in dimensionless fractional-frequency units (Sangria: TDI 1.5 on equal arm lengths; Spritz: TDI 2 on Keplerian orbits), stored as compound HDF5 datasets $\{t, X, Y, Z\}$ at 5 s cadence; read_tdi reads them (and the simulator's group layout) and tdi_to_aet forms $A = (Z - X)/\sqrt{2}$, $E = (X - 2Y + Z)/\sqrt{6}$, $T = (X + Y + Z)/\sqrt{3}$, the orthogonal combinations of the LISA science ground segment conventions [4] (Eq. 40 there), which the LDC follows. The spectral conventions throughout the package are those of the same document, shared with LALSuite (Eqs. 6 and 9): the discrete Fourier transform carries the $\Delta t$ factor, $\tilde x_k = \Delta t \sum_j x_j e^{-2\pi i f_k t_j}$, the PSD is one-sided with $E[\tilde x(f) \tilde x^*(f')] = \tfrac{1}{2} S(f) \delta(f - f')$, and the matched-filter inner product is $4 \, \mathrm{Re} \int \tilde x \tilde y^* / S_n \, \mathrm{d}f$; the second-generation TDI factor $4 \sin^2 2x$ is $|1 - e^{-4ix}|^2$ of $X_2 = (1 - D^4) X_1$ under equal arms (Eq. 39). Their noise is not the strain sensitivity but the TDI noise PSD of the ldc package (ldc_tdi_psd, equal arms, so it does not describe the unequal arms of Spritz): with $x = 2\pi f L/c$, the single-link test-mass and optical-metrology terms in fractional frequency, $S_\mathrm{pm} = S_\mathrm{acc}(f) (2\pi f)^{-4} (2\pi f/c)^2$ and $S_\mathrm{op} = S_\mathrm{oms}(f) (2\pi f/c)^2$, give

\[S_A = S_E = 8 \sin^2 x \left[ 2 S_\mathrm{pm} (3 + 2\cos x + \cos 2x) + S_\mathrm{op} (2 + \cos x) \right],\]

with the noise levels of the named model ("sangria": 7.9 pm, 2.4 fm s⁻²; "SciRDv1": 15 pm, 3 fm s⁻²), a factor $4 \sin^2 2x$ for second-generation TDI, and an optional galactic-confusion term with the package's observation-time fit (ldc_confusion_psd). The port reproduces the package's doctest of the X-channel PSD to eight digits.

Two anchors validate the port against the Sangria training product (tests that run when MILLIHERTZQML_LDC_DIR points at it): the optimal A-channel SNR of catalogue source 4 against the SciRDv1 model, 1883.5 here against 1885.7 from a reference evaluation with a lisabeta waveform (the segment excludes the neighbouring merger of source 3), and a noise-only null test in which the observed record minus every truth stream has a Welch PSD within 15 % of the "sangria" model from 0.1 mHz to 80 mHz and, whitened by that model, unit band powers.

scripts/label_ldc.jl builds point-wise labels from a truth stream: the A channel of the signal-only TDI is scanned with a windowed matched filter against the analytic PSD (windowed_snr), mergers come from the catalogue's coalescence times or, without a catalogue (the blind set's unblinded MBHB stream), from the SNR peaks (snr_peaks), and the positive span is either the paper's fixed window $[t_c - 4\,\mathrm{d}, t_c + 27\,\mathrm{min}]$ (fixed_spans) or the detectable span of the simulator's convention (detectable_spans). Neighbouring mergers (Sangria sources 2, 3, 4 within four days) produce one merged label run, which event-level metrics count as one event. With fixed spans the event table also records each event's signal onset (signal_onsets), the first window inside its span, and past the preceding event's span, whose SNR reaches label_snr_threshold; alerts of a streamed replay are credited from it (see Telemetry Coupling).

Known physical deficiencies

Retained so that the documentation reflects the code as it stands; these are the open items.

  1. Waveform and response scope. IMRPhenomA is non-spinning and dominant-mode only; spins, higher harmonics and precession are not modelled. Under the default sky-averaged response the injected strain is the sensitivity-referred equivalent, without antenna pattern or orbital modulation; the constellation response of the extension supplies the long-wavelength patterns, the Doppler phase and the transfer roll-off, but neither the full TDI transfer function nor a noise model of the null channel.
  2. LDC noise model scope. The equal-arm analytic TDI PSD ignores the arm-length variation of the orbits and the anti-aliasing filters of the LDC simulator (the null test shows a 15 % excess below 3 mHz and the filter roll-off above 80 mHz), and it vanishes at the TDI null $f = c/(2L) \approx 60$ mHz, where the data keep a finite floor: whitening by it (psd = "ldc") is ill-conditioned there and the default high band, which reaches 100 mHz, must then be cut below the null. The Welch estimate (psd = "welch") absorbs all three effects and is the preferred whitening for LDC records. Spritz glitches and gaps are not handled.
  3. Non-stationary foreground. The Galactic confusion noise is modulated over the year by the rotation of the constellation. A single PSD per record whitens it correctly only on average, the trailing estimate of a streamed replay follows it with the delay of its averaging interval, and the noise score of a classifier trained on one year follows the modulation; no time-dependent noise model is implemented.
