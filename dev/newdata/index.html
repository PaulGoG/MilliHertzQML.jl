New data sets

This page describes how a classifier trained on one record is applied to another: a later stretch of the same mission, a record of another data challenge, or a background record without coalescences.

Why an operating point does not transfer

A training run carries two fitted quantities beside the parameters of the classifier. The feature scaler maps the per-feature quantiles of the training partition onto the encoding interval $[0, \phi]$; the decision threshold is fitted on the calibration block to a false-alarm target ([training] threshold_criterion, target_far_per_30d). Both are statements about the training noise. Applied to a record whose noise differs, the scaler places that noise elsewhere on the encoding interval, and the threshold no longer corresponds to the false-alarm rate it was fitted to.

The LDC-2b (Spritz) records show the effect. The scaler fitted on Sangria placed the Spritz noise near its lower bound in three of the eight features: the whitened band powers of Spritz lie below those of Sangria in the upper bands, where the Galactic foreground of Spritz has no forest of resolved binaries. One run in four fell silent on every record (Instrumental glitches). A new record therefore needs the scaler and the threshold fitted on its own noise before its alerts can be read. The parameters of the classifier stay those of the training run.

The procedure

  1. Describe the record in a configuration under configs/experiments/: the HDF5 object of the record ([preprocessing] tdi_group), or, for a product shipped as component streams, the list of components summed into the record ([preprocessing] tdi_components); the sampling rate ([preprocessing] sample_rate); the TDI generation ([ldc] tdi2); the output prefix of the products ([preprocessing] output_prefix); and the [calibration] section. The record itself is passed on the command line with --h5-file.

  2. Label the record if it carries coalescences, from its truth stream, with the signal onsets against the record's own noise:

    julia scripts/label_ldc.jl configs/experiments/<config>.toml --h5-file <record>.h5

    A record without a catalogue of coalescences is a background record; every alarm on it is false, and this step is skipped.

  3. Pre-process the record. The features are whitened by the Welch estimate of the record itself, so the conditioning is already the record's own:

    julia scripts/preprocess_ldc.jl configs/experiments/<config>.toml --h5-file <record>.h5 \
    [--label-file data/inputs/<record>_labels.csv]

  4. Score the record as it comes, with the scaler and threshold of the training run. This is the transfer check, the baseline against which the calibration is judged:

    julia scripts/infer.jl configs/experiments/<config>.toml --run-id <ID> \
    --model models/run_<RUN_ID>/gw_model.jld2 \
    --features data/inputs/<record>_features.csv --labels ""

  5. Calibrate. The first [calibration] block_fraction of the record's windows (0.3 by default) is the calibration block; on it the stage fits the scaler by the rule [calibration] scaler (match, the default; refit; keep) and then the threshold, under the criterion and target of [training]:

    julia scripts/calibrate.jl configs/experiments/<config>.toml --run-id <CAL> \
    --model models/run_<RUN_ID>/gw_model.jld2 \
    --features data/inputs/<record>_features.csv \
    [--labels data/inputs/<record>_labels.csv]

  6. Score the evaluation block, the rest of the record after a buffer of one window length, at the new operating point (with --labels data/inputs/<record>_labels.csv for a labelled record):

    julia scripts/infer.jl configs/experiments/<config>.toml --run-id <CAL> \
    --features data/inputs/<record>_features.csv --labels "" --block evaluation

  7. Report, on the evaluation block alone: the alerts, runs of [telemetry] alert_persistence alarmed windows consecutive in the record, and the confirmations, runs of alert_confirmation_persistence (eleven) alarmed windows, the first and last of which share no sample, both per 30 days; the alert time of every labelled coalescence relative to its merger; the realised false-alarm rate against the one fitted on the calibration block (fit_false_alarms_per_30d in calibration.toml); and the number of false-alarm episodes that fit rests on (fit_false_alarm_episodes), whose inverse square root is its relative error. The stage warns when that number falls below [training] min_fit_episodes.

The threshold is fitted over the candidate grid of the training stage, n_candidates quantiles of the block's scores, extended by [calibration] tail_candidates (200 by default) candidates log-spaced into the upper tail down to one window. On a block without signal the quantile step, a quarter of a percent of the windows, spans the whole range of false-alarm rates an alert trigger is fitted to: the next candidate below the maximum score already alarms more windows than the target admits, and the fit would rest on a single episode at the maximum. The tail candidates make the rates between one episode and that step reachable.

The calibration block and the evaluation block are disjoint: the operating point is fitted on the first and judged on the second, as the training run fits its threshold on the validation block and judges it on the test block.

The scaler rules

match (the default, match_scaler) encodes the noise of the new record where the training noise was encoded. For every feature $j$ two quantiles $p_1 < p_2$ of the noise windows are taken ([calibration] noise_quantiles, by default the median and the 0.99 quantile): $q_\mathrm{ref}(p)$ of the noise windows of the parent's training block, and $q_\mathrm{new}(p)$ of the noise windows of the calibration block. Under the parent's scaler, with bounds $[\ell_j, u_j]$ and encoding span $\phi$, the training quantiles receive the angles

\[a(p) = \phi\,\frac{q_\mathrm{ref}(p) - \ell_j}{u_j - \ell_j},\]

and the new bounds are those of the affine map that sends $q_\mathrm{new}(p_1)$ and $q_\mathrm{new}(p_2)$ to the same angles:

\[u_j' - \ell_j' = \phi\,\frac{q_\mathrm{new}(p_2) - q_\mathrm{new}(p_1)}{a(p_2) - a(p_1)}, \qquad \ell_j' = q_\mathrm{new}(p_1) - a(p_1)\,\frac{u_j' - \ell_j'}{\phi}.\]

If the new noise is an affine image of the training noise, feature by feature, the map recovers it exactly, and the noise windows of the new record are encoded as those of the training block were. A signal window of the new record then lies above its noise as a training signal lay above the training noise. Only the noise windows enter the fit: the labelled positives of a labelled record are excluded from both quantiles. The reference product is [calibration] reference_features and reference_labels, by default the training product of [training], and its training block is read from the split.toml of the parent run.

refit applies the training rule itself, the quantiles [training] scaler_quantiles of the calibration block. That rule was fitted on a training block holding a mixture of noise and signal windows, so it reproduces the training encoding only when the calibration block holds a comparable mixture, as on a labelled record with coalescences; on a background record its upper bound is a quantile of the noise alone.

keep keeps the parent's scaler and refits the threshold only. It measures what the threshold alone repairs.

The calibration run directory

<models>/run_<CAL> holds:

  • gw_model.jld2: the parent's parameters with the new scaler; its metadata gains a calibration entry (run identifier, parent model, feature table, scaler rule);
  • threshold.toml: the threshold fitted on the calibration block, with the criterion, the targets and the rates of the block at the threshold (fit_*), block = "calibration", the parent's threshold (parent_threshold) and model (parent_model);
  • threshold_sweep.csv: the event-level operating characteristic of the calibration block;
  • split.toml: the row ranges calibration and evaluation, the buffer_windows between them, n_windows and the feature table, read by scripts/infer.jl --block;
  • calibration.toml: the fitted quantities, the bounds of the parent's and of the new scaler, the counts of noise windows in the reference and calibration blocks, both thresholds, and the fitted false-alarm rate with its episode count;
  • config.toml: the snapshot of the [model] of the parent, the window geometry and channels of the record, and the [calibration] and threshold rules applied, with provenance; and manifest_snapshot.toml.

The stage refuses the product of a record with gaps, since its blocks take consecutive rows as consecutive windows, and a feature table of another channel set than the model was trained on.

Worked cases

Spritz

The LDC-2b records were scored as they came, with the scalers and thresholds of the Sangria runs, and the comparison at a matched rate of glitch alerts moved the threshold of every run post hoc to where the year of verification binaries raises a given number of alerts per 30 days (Instrumental glitches). The calibration stage is the configuration-driven form of that step, with the scaler refitted as well and the operating point judged on a block it was not fitted on.

Yorsh

The LDC-1b (Yorsh) record holds two years sampled at 5 s, in second-generation TDI, without gaps or glitches. It is shipped as thirteen component streams, which the pre-processing sums into the record ([preprocessing] tdi_components): instrument noise, confusion noise, eight stellar-origin binary black holes and three extreme-mass-ratio inspirals. It holds no massive black hole binary, so it is a background record for this detector and every alert on it is false: its sources are continuous, and the four that end inside the record (two of the stellar-origin binaries leave the band at days 579 and 683, two of the inspirals plunge at days 183 and 439) stay orders of magnitude below the noise of a single window. The configurations configs/experiments/q8_b6_s001_yorsh.toml and q8_b6_s001_ae_yorsh.toml run it under the streaming configuration, on the A channel and on the A and E network; the pre-processing of the composite record takes about two and a half minutes per mode and gives 126,021 windows (729 days).

The transfer check. At the Sangria operating point the circuit is silent: no alarmed window in 729 days on A at any seed, none on the network at three seeds and 22 isolated ones at the fourth. The controls raise between 0 and 0.2 alerts per 30 days (the network of six the most). The cause is the one measured on Spritz. The Yorsh noise whitens to band powers of about 1 with a spread of 0.09 dex, while the Sangria training noise carries excess power in the bands above 1 mHz (medians 1.2 to 2.3) that the smoothed whitening leaves in the features; under the Sangria scaler the median of the Yorsh noise lies between a tenth and a sixth of the encoding interval in those bands, where the Sangria noise lay between a quarter and a third, and its 0.99 quantile stays below the middle where Sangria's reached the top. Nothing enters the region the circuit learned to alarm on.

Calibration. Each of the 32 runs was calibrated on the first 30 % of the record (219 days) under the three scaler rules at the training target of 3 false-alarm episodes per 30 days, and scored on the evaluation block (510 days). Every fit reached the target (21 episodes, 2.88 per 30 days). With the noise matched by its quantiles, the fitted threshold of the circuit returns to the Sangria one to four decimals (0.6305 against 0.6305 on A at the first seed; the controls within 0.01 to 0.05): the Yorsh noise, encoded where the Sangria noise was, produces the same score tail at the same threshold. The kept scaler needs the threshold moved far down (0.39 for the same run), and the refitted scaler far up (0.74); the refit disables the logistic regression altogether, whose probabilities saturate once the noise is stretched over the whole interval.

The realised episode rate on the evaluation block, 3.5 to 6.5 per 30 days, exceeds the fitted 2.88: the tail of the Yorsh noise is not stationary. Counted in 30-day bins at a fixed threshold, the episodes come in stretches about half a year apart (days 60 to 120, 240 to 300, 420 to 510 and 600 to 660), the second year's stronger than the first's, so that the first 219 days hold 20 episodes where the remaining 510 hold 80 to 90. A calibration block shorter than the modulation, or a threshold held fixed across it, therefore misstates the rate by a factor of about two; the streamed replay, which re-estimates its whitening from the trailing month, is the form that follows such a modulation. The persistence tiers absorb the excess: at the matched operating point the circuit raises 0.35 to 0.82 alerts per 30 days on the two channel sets, the controls 0.8 to 1.5, and no run of the 96 confirms a single alert in 510 days. The refitted scaler gives the circuit 0 to 0.12 alerts per 30 days at 1.5 to 2.8 episodes, the kept scaler 0.3 to 1.2 alerts at 4 to 6 episodes.

Two numbers of this record carry over to the procedure. A background record cannot be calibrated on the quantile grid alone: the candidates added to the tail are what let the fit reach a target below a quarter of a percent of the windows. And the matched scaler, not the threshold, is what transfers the operating point: it recovers the Sangria threshold on a record whose raw features sit elsewhere, and the threshold alone, kept or moved, does not.
